Discovering your Dallas-Fort Worth home has code violations can feel overwhelming, especially when you're trying to sell. Whether you've received notices from the city about structural issues, unpermitted additions, or safety hazards, you're probably wondering what your options are and how these violations will affect your ability to sell.

The good news is that selling a house with code violations in Texas is absolutely possible, and you have more options than you might think. Let's walk through what you need to know as a DFW homeowner facing this situation.

Understanding Code Violations in Texas

Code violations occur when your property doesn't meet local building codes, zoning regulations, or safety standards. In the Dallas-Fort Worth area, each municipality has its own code enforcement department with specific regulations.

Common Code Violations in DFW Homes

  • Structural issues like foundation problems or roof damage
  • Unpermitted additions, garages, or room conversions
  • Electrical or plumbing work done without permits
  • Overgrown yards or property maintenance violations
  • Fence height or placement issues
  • HOA violations that escalate to city code enforcement
  • Fire safety hazards or blocked exits
  • HVAC systems not up to current code

Cities like Plano, Frisco, and McKinney have particularly active code enforcement departments. If you've received a notice, it's important to understand the timeline and potential consequences.

What Happens If You Don't Fix Code Violations?

Texas municipalities take code violations seriously, and ignoring them can lead to escalating problems. Here's what you might face:

First, you'll typically receive a written notice giving you a specific timeframe to correct the violation—usually 10 to 30 days depending on the severity and the city. If you don't respond or fix the issue, the city can issue fines ranging from $50 to $2,000 per day for each violation.

In extreme cases, cities in Texas can place a lien on your property for unpaid fines or even pursue criminal misdemeanor charges for continued violations. Some municipalities may also obtain a court order to enter your property and make the repairs themselves, then bill you for the work plus administrative costs.

Impact on Your Texas Homestead Exemption

While your homestead exemption protects your primary residence from most creditors in Texas, it doesn't protect you from liens placed by the government for code violations. These liens can attach to your property and must be paid when you sell.

Can You Sell a House With Active Code Violations in Texas?

Yes, you absolutely can sell a house with code violations in Texas, but you need to understand your disclosure obligations and how violations affect the sales process.

Texas Disclosure Requirements

Texas law requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice for residential property. You must disclose any known code violations or notices you've received from the city. Failing to disclose this information can lead to legal liability after the sale, potentially costing you much more than addressing the issue upfront.

The disclosure requirement is strict in Texas, and courts have consistently ruled in favor of buyers who weren't informed about code violations. Transparency is not just legally required—it's your best protection.

Your Options for Selling a House With Code Violations in DFW

As a Dallas-Fort Worth homeowner with code violations, you have several paths forward. Let's explore each option and what it means for your situation.

Option 1: Fix the Violations Before Selling

This is the traditional route, but it comes with significant considerations. You'll need to hire licensed contractors, pull the proper permits, and get final inspections approved. In the DFW area, this process can take weeks or months depending on contractor availability and inspection schedules.

For unpermitted work, you may need to hire an engineer to certify the work was done correctly, which can cost $500 to $2,000 just for the inspection. Some violations, like unpermitted additions, might need to be brought up to current code, which could be significantly more expensive than the original work.

The upside is that you'll be able to list your home on the traditional market and potentially get top dollar. The downside is the time, stress, and upfront costs you'll need to invest.

Option 2: Negotiate Repairs With the Buyer

Some buyers are willing to purchase a home with code violations if you reduce the price or offer credits at closing. However, there's a major obstacle with this approach: most mortgage lenders won't approve loans for properties with active code violations or open permits.

This means you'll likely be limited to cash buyers or investors who can look past the violations. Traditional retail buyers using FHA, VA, or conventional financing will typically walk away once violations are discovered during the inspection or title search.

Costs of Fixing Common Code Violations in DFW

If you're considering fixing violations before selling, here are typical costs in the Dallas-Fort Worth area:

  • Permit fees: $50-$500 depending on the scope of work and city
  • Electrical upgrades: $1,500-$5,000 for panel upgrades or rewiring
  • Plumbing corrections: $800-$3,000 for typical violations
  • Structural repairs: $2,000-$20,000+ depending on severity
  • Engineering inspections: $500-$2,000 per report
  • Contractor labor: Generally 20-30% higher in hot DFW markets like Frisco and Southlake
  • Bringing unpermitted work up to code: Often 50-100% of the original project cost

These costs add up quickly, and they don't include the opportunity cost of holding the property longer while repairs are completed and inspected.

HOA Violations and Code Enforcement in Texas

Texas has some of the most powerful HOA laws in the country, and many DFW communities have very active homeowners associations. In areas like The Colony, Prosper, and Celina, HOA violations can escalate to city code enforcement issues.

If your HOA has placed a lien on your property for unpaid fines, this must be resolved at closing. Texas law allows HOAs to foreclose on homes for unpaid assessments and fines, though this is typically a last resort.

When selling a house with both code violations and HOA issues, working with a cash buyer who understands these complexities can save you considerable headaches.

The Title Company's Role

In Texas, title companies play a crucial role in real estate transactions. During the title search, any liens from code violation fines or HOA assessments will be discovered. These must typically be paid from your proceeds at closing.

If violations are severe enough, the title company may refuse to insure the title until they're resolved. This can kill a traditional sale, but cash buyers often work with title companies experienced in handling these situations.

Tax Implications of Selling With Code Violations

One advantage of being a Texas homeowner is that there's no state income tax on your home sale proceeds. However, you'll still need to consider federal capital gains taxes if applicable.

If you've lived in your home as your primary residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years, you can exclude up to $250,000 in gains ($500,000 if married filing jointly) from federal taxes. This exclusion applies regardless of whether your home has code violations.

Keep in mind that any money you spend fixing code violations can be added to your cost basis, potentially reducing your capital gains tax liability.
