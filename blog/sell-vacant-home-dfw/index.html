How to Sell a Vacant Home in DFW

Published June 17, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Owning a vacant home in the Dallas-Fort Worth area comes with unique challenges—from rising property taxes to maintenance costs and security concerns. Whether you inherited the property, relocated for work, or simply moved on to a new home, selling your vacant house quickly can save you thousands of dollars and considerable stress.

Understanding the Challenges of Vacant Properties in Texas

Vacant homes in DFW face specific risks that occupied properties do not. Without regular occupancy, these houses are more vulnerable to break-ins, vandalism, and code violations. In cities like Plano, Frisco, and McKinney, homeowners associations may fine you for unmaintained yards or exterior issues. Texas property taxes continue accumulating whether your home is occupied or not, and in high-value areas like Southlake, Colleyville, and Grapevine, these taxes can be substantial.

Insurance companies often charge higher premiums for vacant properties or may require specialized vacant home insurance. Some insurers will only cover a home that has been vacant for 30-60 days under a standard policy. Additionally, without climate control, Texas heat can cause significant damage—warped floors, cracked foundations, and pest infestations become more likely in our extreme weather conditions.

Texas-Specific Considerations When You Sell Vacant Home DFW

Texas law requires sellers to complete a Seller Disclosure Notice, even for vacant properties. You must disclose known defects and material facts about the property. For inherited homes, you may need to navigate probate court proceedings before selling, which can add months to the timeline. In Tarrant, Dallas, Collin, and Denton Counties, property transfer fees and taxes apply, though Texas has no state capital gains tax on real estate sales.

Many DFW cities have specific ordinances regarding vacant properties. Cities like Arlington, Grand Prairie, and Garland require owners to register vacant properties and maintain them to code standards. Failure to comply can result in fines ranging from $500 to $2,000. The property must have utilities functioning for showings, adding to your monthly costs even while the home sits empty.

Should You Renovate Before Selling Your Vacant Home?

The DFW market remains competitive, but renovating a vacant home is not always necessary or profitable. Buyers in Richardson, Allen, and Carrollton often expect move-in ready homes, but renovations can cost $20,000-$50,000 or more and take months to complete. During this time, you continue paying taxes, insurance, utilities, and HOA fees. Unless you have significant equity and time to invest, traditional listing may drain your resources.

Many homeowners discover that selling as-is to a cash buyer eliminates renovation costs entirely. This approach works especially well for properties needing foundation repairs—a common issue in North Texas due to our clay soil—or homes with outdated systems, roof damage, or cosmetic issues.
