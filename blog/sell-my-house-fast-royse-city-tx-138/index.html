Texas-Specific Considerations for Landlords

When selling rental property in Texas, you'll want to understand several state-specific factors. Texas has no state income tax, which means your proceeds from the sale won't be subject to state-level taxation—though federal capital gains tax may still apply depending on your situation.

If the property was previously your primary residence and you claimed the Texas homestead exemption, you'll want to ensure that exemption was properly removed when you converted it to a rental. Property taxes on investment properties in Rockwall County can be significantly higher than homesteaded properties, which is one reason many landlords find their rental income isn't stretching as far as expected.

Additionally, Texas community property laws mean that if you're married, your spouse will generally need to be involved in the sale process even if only your name is on the deed. This is important to understand upfront to avoid delays during closing.

Texas Foreclosure and Financial Hardship Considerations

If you're facing foreclosure on your Royse City rental property, time is critical. Texas follows a non-judicial foreclosure process, which means lenders can foreclose relatively quickly—often in as little as 60-90 days from the first missed payment.

Unlike your primary residence, rental properties don't qualify for Texas homestead exemption protections, making them more vulnerable to foreclosure proceedings. If you're behind on payments, selling quickly to a cash buyer may be your best option to avoid foreclosure, protect your credit, and potentially walk away with some equity.
