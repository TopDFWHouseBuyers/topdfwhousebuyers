Selling a House With Tenants in Texas - Landlord Guide

Published October 12, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Selling a rental property while tenants are still living there can feel overwhelming, especially in the competitive Dallas-Fort Worth market. As a landlord in Texas, you have specific rights and responsibilities that impact how you can market and sell your property. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know about selling a house with tenants in Texas, from lease considerations to your best exit strategies.

Understanding Texas Tenant Rights When Selling

Texas law provides specific protections for tenants, even when a landlord decides to sell. If your tenants have an active lease agreement, that lease typically transfers to the new owner. You cannot simply terminate a lease early because you want to sell, unless the lease includes a specific clause allowing early termination with proper notice.

In Texas, you must provide reasonable notice before showing the property to potential buyers. While Texas law doesn't specify an exact timeframe, 24 hours is generally considered reasonable and professional. Your lease agreement may spell out specific notice requirements for property access.

Month-to-Month vs. Fixed-Term Leases

If your tenants are on a month-to-month lease, you have more flexibility. Texas law requires you to provide at least 30 days written notice before terminating a month-to-month tenancy. With a fixed-term lease, you'll need to honor the lease until it expires or negotiate an early termination agreement with your tenants.

Three Options for Selling Your Rental Property

When selling a house with tenants in Texas, you have three main approaches to consider based on your timeline and situation.

Option 1: Sell With Tenants in Place

Some investors actually prefer purchasing occupied rental properties with good tenants already in place. This option works best if your tenants pay rent on time, maintain the property well, and have a longer-term lease. The property continues generating income during the sale process, and you avoid vacancy costs. However, coordinating showings can be challenging, and the buyer pool is limited to investors rather than owner-occupants.

Option 2: Wait Until the Lease Expires

If your lease is ending soon, waiting might make sense. Once tenants move out, you can prepare the property for sale, make repairs, and appeal to both investors and families looking for a primary residence. The downside is you'll need to continue managing the property and may face vacancy periods that cost you money.

Texas-Specific Considerations for Landlords

Selling rental property in Texas comes with unique advantages and considerations that differ from other states.

No State Income Tax Benefits

Texas doesn't impose state income tax on capital gains from property sales. This means you'll only deal with federal capital gains taxes, potentially keeping more profit from your sale compared to landlords in states like California or New York.

Security Deposit Requirements

Under Texas Property Code, security deposits must be returned within 30 days of tenant move-out, along with an itemized list of any deductions. When selling with tenants in place, these deposits typically transfer to the new owner. Make sure this is clearly documented in your sales contract to protect yourself from future liability.

Common Challenges When Selling With Tenants

Even with cooperative tenants, selling an occupied property presents unique obstacles. Tenants may not keep the property show-ready, might resist frequent showings, or could feel uncertain about their housing future. Some tenants intentionally make showings difficult if they're unhappy about the sale.

In the competitive DFW market, homes in Southlake, Colleyville, and other desirable areas sell quickly when they show well. A tenant-occupied property often can't compete with vacant, staged homes, potentially extending your time on market and reducing your sale price.
