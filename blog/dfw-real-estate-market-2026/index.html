DFW Real Estate Market 2026 - What Sellers Need to Know

The Dallas-Fort Worth real estate market continues to evolve in 2026, presenting both opportunities and challenges for homeowners looking to sell. Understanding current market conditions, Texas-specific regulations, and your selling options can help you make the best decision for your situation.

Current DFW Market Conditions in 2026

The DFW real estate market in 2026 reflects a more balanced environment compared to the frenzy of previous years. While the region continues to attract new residents drawn by job growth and no state income tax, inventory levels have normalized in many areas including Plano, Frisco, Allen, and McKinney. Days on market have increased slightly, giving buyers more time to make decisions and sellers more reason to price strategically.

Interest rates remain a key factor influencing buyer behavior. Many potential buyers are still adjusting to higher borrowing costs compared to the historic lows of 2020-2021. This means homes need to be competitively priced and in good condition to attract serious offers. Areas like Southlake, Colleyville, and Keller continue to see strong demand due to excellent schools and amenities, while neighborhoods in Arlington, Grand Prairie, and Garland offer more affordable entry points for first-time buyers.

Texas Seller Disclosure Requirements in 2026

Texas law requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice for most residential transactions. This legally binding document, governed by Section 5.008 of the Texas Property Code, requires you to disclose known material defects affecting the property. In 2026, buyers are increasingly aware of their rights, making accurate disclosure more important than ever.

You must disclose issues like foundation problems, roof damage, plumbing issues, or past flooding. Richardson, Carrollton, and parts of Lewisville have areas prone to drainage concerns, making water-related disclosures particularly important. Failure to disclose known defects can result in legal action even after closing. If you're uncertain about your property's condition or concerned about disclosure liability, selling to a cash buyer who purchases as-is can eliminate these worries entirely.

What It Costs to Sell a House in DFW

Selling through traditional channels in the DFW area involves several expenses. Real estate agent commissions typically total 5-6% of the sale price—that means $15,000-$18,000 on a $300,000 home. Title and closing costs add another 1-3%, and Texas sellers often contribute to buyer closing costs in competitive negotiations.

Many DFW homes built in the 1970s-1990s throughout Mesquite, Hurst, Bedford, and Euless need updates before listing. Pre-sale repairs and cosmetic improvements can easily cost $10,000-$30,000 or more. Factor in staging, professional photography, and potential price reductions if your home sits on the market, and your net proceeds can be significantly less than expected.
