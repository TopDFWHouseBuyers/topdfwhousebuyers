Current State of the DFW Real Estate Market in 2026

The Dallas-Fort Worth housing market has matured considerably since the explosive growth years of the early 2020s. In 2026, we're seeing a more balanced market across most DFW communities, including Plano, Frisco, McKinney, Arlington, and surrounding cities.

Inventory levels have increased compared to previous years, giving buyers more options while also meaning sellers face more competition. Days on market have extended in many neighborhoods, particularly for homes that aren't priced competitively or need significant repairs. However, well-maintained homes in desirable areas like Southlake, Colleyville, and Prosper continue to sell relatively quickly.

Interest rates have stabilized but remain higher than the historic lows of 2020-2021, which affects buyer purchasing power and overall demand. This creates a more selective buyer pool focused on homes that offer genuine value.

Texas-Specific Considerations for Sellers in 2026

Selling a home in Texas comes with unique legal and financial considerations that differ from other states. Understanding these factors helps you plan appropriately and avoid surprises during the selling process.

Texas Property Tax and Homestead Exemption

If you've been claiming the homestead exemption on your DFW property, you'll need to notify your county appraisal district after selling. This exemption, which provides significant tax savings, terminates when you no longer occupy the home as your primary residence. Many sellers in Collin County, Denton County, Tarrant County, and Dallas County overlook this notification requirement.

Texas property taxes remain a significant consideration for buyers, which affects your home's marketability. Homes in areas with lower tax rates or strong school districts often attract more buyer interest.

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, meaning both spouses typically must consent to the sale of a home, even if only one name appears on the title. If you're married and selling property in Richardson, Allen, Garland, or anywhere in DFW, ensure both spouses are prepared to sign necessary documents. This becomes particularly important in divorce situations or when dealing with inherited property.

HOA Rules and Regulations

Many DFW neighborhoods, particularly in newer developments in Frisco, McKinney, Flower Mound, and Lewisville, have active homeowners associations. Before listing, review your HOA requirements regarding property condition, exterior modifications, and signage restrictions. Outstanding HOA dues must typically be paid at closing, and some associations require transfer fees.

Texas Foreclosure Timeline and Process

Texas has one of the fastest foreclosure timelines in the nation, with non-judicial foreclosures possible in as little as 41 days after the first missed payment. If you're facing foreclosure in Grand Prairie, Mesquite, Carrollton, or anywhere in DFW, understanding this aggressive timeline is critical. Texas law requires lenders to send a notice of default and give homeowners at least 20 days to cure the default before accelerating the loan.

Pricing Your Home Correctly in 2026

Accurate pricing has become more critical in the 2026 DFW market. Overpriced homes sit longer, eventually requiring price reductions that can stigmatize the listing and result in lower final sale prices than if priced correctly initially.

Recent comparable sales in your specific Coppell, Arlington, or Plano neighborhood provide the best pricing guidance. Factor in your home's condition honestly—today's buyers have access to extensive online information and quickly eliminate overpriced properties from consideration.

If your home needs significant work, pricing must account for repair costs buyers will face. Many sellers discover that after factoring in needed repairs, holding costs, and commission fees, their net proceeds from a traditional sale may not exceed a cash offer.

Hidden Costs of Selling Traditionally

Many DFW sellers underestimate the total costs of traditional home sales:

  • Real estate commissions (5-6% of sale price)
  • Closing costs (1-3% of sale price)
  • Pre-listing repairs and improvements
  • Staging costs
  • Continuing mortgage payments during listing period
  • Property taxes and insurance until closing
  • HOA dues through closing date
  • Utilities for showings and open houses
  • Potential buyer-requested repairs after inspection

For a $350,000 home, these costs can easily exceed $30,000-$40,000, reducing net proceeds significantly.

Special Situations Affecting DFW Sellers in 2026

Inherited Properties

Inheriting property in Texas involves probate unless the property was held in a trust. The probate process can take several months, during which property taxes, maintenance, and insurance continue accruing. Many heirs across DFW choose to sell inherited homes quickly to avoid these ongoing expenses, especially when the property needs repairs or updates.

Divorce Situations

Texas community property laws make home sales during divorce more complex. Both spouses must typically agree on the sale terms and sign closing documents. Cash sales often appeal to divorcing couples because they eliminate the uncertainty of traditional sales and provide quick resolution.

Job Relocation

DFW's strong economy means job relocations remain common. If you're transferring to Houston, Austin, or out of state, the speed and certainty of a cash sale can align better with relocation timelines than waiting for traditional buyer financing.
