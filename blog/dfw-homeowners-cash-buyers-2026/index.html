Texas-Specific Laws That Protect Homeowners

Understanding your rights under Texas law is essential when working with cash buyers. The Lone Star State offers several protections that differentiate it from other markets.

Texas Homestead Exemption

Texas provides one of the most generous homestead exemptions in the nation. This law protects your primary residence from most creditors, though property taxes, home equity loans, and mortgage liens are exceptions. When selling to a cash buyer, your homestead exemption rights don't accelerate the sale—you still maintain control over timing and terms.

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, meaning that if you're married, your spouse generally has an ownership interest in your home regardless of whose name appears on the deed. Any legitimate cash buyer will require both spouses to sign the sales contract and closing documents. Be wary of any buyer who doesn't request spousal consent—this is a red flag for potential fraud.

Texas Real Estate License Requirements

In Texas, anyone who buys and sells real estate as a business must hold a valid real estate license issued by the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC). Foreclosure Timeline in Texas

Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state with one of the fastest foreclosure processes in the country. From the first missed payment to foreclosure sale, the timeline can be as short as 60 days, though it typically takes 90 to 120 days. If you're facing foreclosure in cities like Mesquite, Grand Prairie, or Irving, selling to a cash buyer can help you avoid foreclosure's long-term credit impact—but you must act quickly.

What to Expect From Legitimate Cash Buyers

Not all cash buyers operate the same way. Understanding what separates professional, licensed buyers from less scrupulous operators helps you navigate the process safely.

Transparent Pricing

Reputable cash buyers provide clear explanations of how they calculate offers. While cash offers typically range from 70% to 85% of a home's after-repair value (minus repair costs), the exact percentage depends on your property's condition, location, and market factors specific to your DFW neighborhood.

A home in established Plano or Frisco may command a higher percentage than a property needing significant repairs in older sections of Garland or Mesquite. This isn't unfair—it reflects renovation costs, holding costs, and resale potential.

No Obligation Offers

Legitimate cash buyers never pressure you to accept an offer immediately. You should have time to review any proposal, consult with family members or an attorney, and compare it to other options like listing with a real estate agent or making repairs yourself.

Proof of Funds

Before you commit to a cash buyer, request proof of funds. This documentation—typically a bank statement or letter from a financial institution—confirms the buyer actually has the cash to close. This protects you from wasting time with buyers who can't complete the transaction.

Clear Communication About Costs

In traditional Texas real estate transactions, sellers typically pay 6% in realtor commissions plus additional closing costs totaling 1-3% of the sale price. When selling to a cash buyer, you should understand exactly what costs you'll incur. Many cash buyers cover all closing costs, meaning the offer you receive is the amount you'll actually walk away with—though this varies by company.

Red Flags to Watch For

While many cash buyers operate ethically, some use predatory practices. Protect yourself by watching for these warning signs:

  • Pressure tactics: Buyers who insist you sign immediately without time to review documents
  • Upfront fees: Legitimate buyers don't charge fees to make offers
  • No physical inspection: Professional buyers always inspect properties before finalizing offers
  • Unusually low offers: Offers significantly below market value without clear justification
  • No TREC license: Buyers operating without proper Texas licensing
  • Unclear contracts: Agreements with confusing terms or hidden clauses

The Cash Buyer Process: Step by Step

Understanding the typical process helps you know what to expect when working with cash buyers in the DFW area.

Step 1: Initial Contact

You reach out by phone or through a website form, providing basic information about your property and situation.

Step 2: Property Evaluation

The cash buyer schedules a convenient time to view your home. This typically takes 20-30 minutes and involves photographing the property and noting its condition.

Step 3: Offer Presentation

Within 24-48 hours, you receive a written cash offer with clear terms. Reputable buyers explain how they calculated the offer and answer your questions.

Step 4: Contract Signing

If you accept the offer, you'll sign a purchase agreement. In Texas, this contract should include contingencies for title review and, if married, must be signed by both spouses.

Step 5: Title Work

A title company researches your property's title, identifies any liens or issues, and prepares closing documents. This typically takes 7-14 days.

Step 6: Closing

You meet at a title company office in your area—whether that's Plano, Arlington, Lewisville, or anywhere in the metroplex—to sign final documents and receive your funds, usually via wire transfer or cashier's check.
