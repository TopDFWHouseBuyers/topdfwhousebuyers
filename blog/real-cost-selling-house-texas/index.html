The Real Cost of Selling a House in Texas

Published September 14, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

If you're planning to sell your house in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, understanding the true cost of selling is essential to avoid unwelcome surprises at closing. Many Texas homeowners are shocked to discover that selling a home involves much more than just realtor commissions—there are closing costs, repairs, title fees, and other expenses that can quickly add up. Let's break down exactly what you can expect to pay when selling your house in Texas.

Understanding the Total Cost of Selling Your Texas Home

The cost of selling a house in Texas typically ranges from 8% to 10% of your home's sale price when using traditional methods. For a $300,000 home in Plano or Frisco, that means you could pay between $24,000 and $30,000 in total selling costs. These expenses can significantly impact your net proceeds, so it's important to understand each component.

Real Estate Agent Commissions

The largest expense when selling a home in Texas is typically the real estate commission. In the Dallas-Fort Worth area, total commissions usually range from 5% to 6% of the sale price, split between the listing agent and buyer's agent.

Breaking Down Commission Costs

  • Listing agent commission: 2.5% to 3%
  • Buyer's agent commission: 2.5% to 3%
  • On a $300,000 home: $15,000 to $18,000 in total commissions

While these commissions are technically negotiable, most agents in Plano, McKinney, Southlake, and surrounding areas expect standard rates. Keep in mind that commission is usually deducted from your proceeds at closing.

Texas-Specific Closing Costs for Sellers

Texas has unique closing cost considerations that differ from other states. Understanding these Texas-specific costs helps you budget accurately for your home sale.

Title Policy Costs

In Texas, the seller typically pays for the owner's title insurance policy. This is different from many states where the buyer pays this cost. Title insurance rates in Texas are regulated by the state, and for a $300,000 home in Richardson or Allen, you can expect to pay approximately $1,500 to $2,500 for the owner's title policy.

Property Taxes

Texas property taxes are among the highest in the nation—a trade-off for having no state income tax. As the seller, you'll need to pay your prorated share of property taxes through the closing date. In DFW counties like Collin, Denton, and Tarrant, property tax rates typically range from 2% to 2.5% of your home's assessed value annually.

HOA Fees and Transfer Fees

Many neighborhoods in Frisco, The Colony, Prosper, and Celina have homeowners associations. You'll need to pay:

  • Prorated HOA dues through closing
  • HOA transfer fees (typically $200 to $500)
  • HOA document preparation fees
  • Any outstanding HOA violations or fines

Attorney or Escrow Fees

While not required by law, many Texas home sales involve an attorney or title company to handle closing. These fees typically range from $500 to $1,500 depending on the complexity of your transaction.

Home Repairs and Pre-Sale Improvements

Before listing your home in Grapevine, Keller, or any DFW city, you'll likely need to invest in repairs and improvements to attract buyers and pass inspections.

Common Pre-Sale Expenses

  • Deep cleaning and decluttering: $200 to $500
  • Paint and minor repairs: $1,000 to $3,000
  • Landscaping improvements: $500 to $2,000
  • HVAC servicing (critical in Texas heat): $150 to $500
  • Roof repairs or certification: $500 to $5,000+

In the competitive DFW market, homes in move-in ready condition typically sell faster and for higher prices. However, these improvements require upfront cash that reduces your net proceeds.

Inspection-Related Repairs

After your buyer's inspection, you may need to negotiate repairs or provide credits. Foundation issues are particularly common in North Texas due to our expansive clay soil, and repair costs can range from $3,000 to $15,000 or more. Other common inspection issues in Arlington, Grand Prairie, and Mesquite include:

  • Plumbing repairs: $500 to $3,000
  • Electrical updates: $500 to $2,500
  • HVAC repairs or replacement: $3,000 to $8,000
  • Roof repairs: $1,000 to $10,000

Marketing and Staging Costs

To sell your home quickly in Carrollton, Coppell, or other competitive DFW neighborhoods, you may incur additional marketing expenses:

  • Professional photography: $300 to $600
  • Home staging: $1,500 to $3,500
  • Virtual tours: $200 to $500
  • Curb appeal improvements: $500 to $2,000

While your real estate agent typically handles basic marketing, these premium services often require additional investment.

Texas Community Property Laws and Selling Costs

Texas is a community property state, which affects how selling costs are handled if you're married. Both spouses typically must consent to the sale of a home, and all selling costs come from the community proceeds. If you're selling due to divorce, these costs can become more complicated, and you may need additional legal assistance.

Capital Gains Tax Considerations in Texas

While Texas has no state income tax (which benefits home sellers), you may still owe federal capital gains tax on your profit. Fortunately, the IRS offers a generous exclusion:

  • Single filers can exclude up to $250,000 in profit
  • Married couples filing jointly can exclude up to $500,000
  • You must have lived in the home as your primary residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years

Most homeowners in Lewisville, Flower Mound, or Euless won't owe capital gains tax, but high-value properties in areas like Southlake or Colleyville may exceed these thresholds.

Texas Homestead Exemption and Your Sale

If you've claimed the Texas homestead exemption on your property, you'll need to remove this exemption after selling. While this doesn't cost money, it's an administrative step you shouldn't forget. The homestead exemption provides significant property tax savings while you own the home, but it doesn't transfer to the new owner.

Hidden Costs That Surprise Texas Sellers

Beyond the major expenses, several smaller costs can catch DFW homeowners off guard:

  • Homeowner's insurance until closing: You must maintain coverage through the sale
  • Utilities during vacancy: If you move out before closing, you'll pay utilities on an empty house
  • Storage costs: If you need to store belongings between homes
  • Moving expenses: $1,000 to $5,000 depending on distance and amount
  • Lawn maintenance: Keeping the property presentable during showings
  • Seller concessions: Buyers often request 1% to 3% in closing cost assistance

How to Reduce Your Selling Costs in Texas

If you decide to pursue a traditional sale, consider these strategies to minimize expenses:

  • Negotiate commission rates with agents (though this may limit your agent pool)
  • Handle minor repairs yourself rather than hiring contractors
  • Improve curb appeal with DIY landscaping projects
  • Time your sale strategically to avoid double mortgage payments
  • Price competitively to avoid extended holding costs
  • Request pre-listing inspections to identify issues early

However, remember that every month your house sits on the market in Plano, Frisco, or anywhere in DFW costs you money in mortgage payments, insurance, utilities, taxes, and maintenance.

When Selling Costs Become Overwhelming

Some situations make traditional selling costs particularly challenging:

  • You're behind on mortgage payments and facing foreclosure (Texas has a relatively fast foreclosure timeline)
  • The house needs extensive repairs you can't afford
  • You've inherited a property and don't have cash for improvements
  • You need to relocate quickly for work
  • You're going through a divorce and need to liquidate assets
  • The property has title issues or liens

In these scenarios, the cost of selling a house in Texas through traditional methods may be prohibitive or impossible. A direct cash sale eliminates these barriers and provides certainty in uncertain situations.

Making the Right Choice for Your Situation

Every homeowner's situation is unique. A traditional sale might make sense if:

  • Your home is in excellent condition
  • You have 3-6 months to wait for the right buyer
  • You have cash available for upfront costs
  • The market is strongly favoring sellers

A cash sale might be better if:

  • You need to close quickly
  • Your home needs significant repairs
