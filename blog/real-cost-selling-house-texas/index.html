The Real Cost of Selling a House in Texas

Published May 20, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Thinking about selling your home in the Dallas-Fort Worth area? Understanding the true cost of selling a house in Texas is crucial before you list. Many homeowners are surprised to discover that traditional home sales can cost 8-10% of the sale price or more.

Texas Realtor Commission Costs

The largest expense when selling a house in Texas is typically realtor commissions. In the DFW market, total commission averages 5-6% of the sale price, split between the listing agent and buyer's agent. On a $350,000 home (close to the median price in Plano and Frisco), that equals $17,500-$21,000 right off the top. This is negotiable, but most agents won't reduce their fees significantly in competitive markets like McKinney, Allen, or Southlake.

Texas Title and Closing Costs

Texas is unique because sellers traditionally pay for the owner's title policy, which protects the buyer. In the DFW area, title insurance costs approximately $1,000-$3,000 depending on your home's value. You'll also pay escrow fees, typically $500-$800, and potentially half of the property taxes prorated to the closing date. Unlike some states, Texas has no state transfer tax, which saves sellers money. However, some cities charge minimal transfer fees. Total closing costs for sellers in Richardson, Garland, or Arlington typically run 1-2% of the sale price.

Repairs and Home Preparation Expenses

Most DFW buyers expect move-in ready homes, especially in competitive areas like Grapevine, Colleyville, and Keller. Pre-listing repairs, fresh paint, landscaping, and deep cleaning easily cost $3,000-$15,000. If your buyer requests repairs after inspection (common in Texas), expect to negotiate credits or make additional fixes. Many sellers in The Colony, Lewisville, and Flower Mound spend $5,000-$10,000 addressing inspection items ranging from roof repairs to HVAC maintenance.

Texas-Specific Disclosure Requirements

Texas law requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice covering property condition and defects. While the form itself is free, failure to disclose known issues can result in legal liability after closing. Many Carrollton, Coppell, and Prosper sellers hire inspectors ($300-$500) before listing to identify problems upfront, avoiding post-sale disputes.

Holding Costs During the Sale

The average home in the DFW metroplex takes 30-60 days to sell, though this varies by location and market conditions. During this period, you continue paying your mortgage, property taxes, insurance, and utilities. In higher-priced areas like Celina and Prosper, monthly holding costs can exceed $3,000. If your home sits longer than expected, these costs add up quickly. Factor in homeowner association fees common in master-planned communities throughout North Richland Hills, Bedford, and Euless.

Hidden Costs and Surprise Expenses

Several overlooked expenses impact your net proceeds. Staging can cost $2,000-$5,000 for vacant homes in Grand Prairie, Mesquite, or Arlington. Professional photography runs $200-$400. If you've already moved, you might pay for lawn maintenance and utilities on an empty house. Some HOAs in DFW require architectural approval before listing, potentially delaying your sale. Finally, if you owe more than 97% of your home's value, you may need to bring cash to closing to cover all selling costs.
