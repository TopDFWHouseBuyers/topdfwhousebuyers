Understanding Texas Inheritance Laws and Probate

Before you can sell an inherited property in Keller, you'll need to navigate Texas probate law. Texas is a community property state, which means assets acquired during marriage are typically owned equally by both spouses. This affects how property passes to heirs and who has the legal authority to sell.

The probate process in Texas can take anywhere from a few months to over a year, depending on whether the deceased had a valid will, the complexity of the estate, and whether all heirs agree on the distribution. During this time, you'll need to:

  • File the will with the Tarrant County Probate Court
  • Get appointed as executor or administrator of the estate
  • Notify creditors and settle outstanding debts
  • Obtain Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration
  • Get approval to sell the property if required by the court

If multiple siblings or family members inherited the property together, all parties must agree to the sale and sign the closing documents. This can sometimes create challenges if beneficiaries disagree about timing or price.

Texas-Specific Considerations for Inherited Properties

Homestead Exemption and Property Taxes

In Texas, homestead exemptions can significantly reduce property taxes for owner-occupied residences. However, when you inherit a property in Keller that you don't plan to occupy, you'll lose this exemption. This means property taxes may increase substantially, making it even more important to decide quickly whether to keep or sell the home.

The good news? Texas has no state income tax, which means you won't pay state capital gains taxes on the sale of your inherited property. You may still owe federal capital gains tax, but inherited properties typically receive a 'step-up in basis,' meaning the property's value is reset to its fair market value at the time of the owner's death, potentially reducing or eliminating capital gains tax liability.

Maintaining HOA Compliance

Many Keller neighborhoods, including popular communities like Saddlebrook, Waterford, and Bear Creek, have active homeowners associations. As the new owner of an inherited property, you're responsible for paying HOA dues and maintaining the property according to association standards. Falling behind on these obligations can result in liens against the property and complicate your sale.

Step-by-Step Guide to Selling Your Inherited Keller Property

Step 1: Determine Your Legal Authority to Sell

First, confirm that you have the legal right to sell the property. This typically requires completing the probate process and obtaining the necessary court documentation. If you're one of multiple heirs, you'll need agreement from all parties before proceeding.

Step 2: Assess the Property Condition

Visit the property and honestly evaluate its condition. Many inherited homes in Keller have been owned by elderly family members for decades and may need significant updates or repairs. Common issues include outdated kitchens and bathrooms, old HVAC systems, roof damage, and deferred maintenance.

Properties in established Keller neighborhoods like Indian Springs or Town Center may have good bones but need cosmetic updates to appeal to today's buyers who expect granite countertops, open floor plans, and modern finishes.

Step 3: Decide Whether to Repair or Sell As-Is

This is one of the biggest decisions you'll face. Should you invest money into repairs and updates, or sell the property in its current condition? Consider these factors:

  • Your available cash for renovations
  • Time you can dedicate to managing contractors
  • Current Keller real estate market conditions
  • The property's location and neighborhood desirability
  • Whether you live locally or out of state

Many heirs in Keller choose to sell as-is to avoid the hassle, expense, and time involved in making repairs, especially if they live outside the Dallas-Fort Worth area or are managing the estate from another state.

Step 4: Clear Out Personal Belongings

Inherited properties often contain decades of personal belongings, furniture, and memories. You'll need to sort through everything, decide what to keep, donate, or dispose of, and completely clear the house before selling. This emotional and time-consuming process can take weeks or even months.

Step 5: Choose Your Selling Method

You have several options for selling an inherited property in Keller:

  • Traditional listing with a real estate agent: This route may get you top dollar but requires repairs, staging, showings, and typically takes 60-90 days or longer. You'll also pay 6% in agent commissions plus closing costs.
  • Selling to a cash buyer: This option allows you to sell quickly, as-is, without repairs, showings, or commissions. It's ideal if you need to settle the estate quickly or the property needs significant work.
  • For Sale by Owner (FSBO): This saves on commissions but requires significant time and real estate knowledge to navigate contracts, disclosures, and negotiations.

Tax Implications of Selling Inherited Property in Texas

Understanding the tax implications of selling an inherited property can help you make informed decisions and maximize your proceeds.

Step-Up in Basis

When you inherit property, the IRS allows a 'step-up in basis,' meaning the property's cost basis is adjusted to its fair market value on the date of the deceased owner's death. This can significantly reduce or eliminate capital gains taxes when you sell. For example, if your parent bought a Keller home in 1995 for $150,000 and it's worth $450,000 when you inherit it, your basis is $450,000, not the original purchase price.

No Texas State Income Tax

Texas is one of the few states with no state income tax, meaning you won't owe state-level capital gains taxes on your sale. You may still owe federal capital gains tax if you sell for more than the stepped-up basis, but the Texas tax advantage makes selling inherited property here more financially favorable than in many other states.

Property Tax Considerations

Once you inherit the property, you're responsible for property taxes going forward. In Keller, property taxes are assessed by Tarrant County and fund local schools, infrastructure, and services. Without the homestead exemption, you'll pay the full tax amount, which can be substantial. Selling quickly can minimize your property tax liability.
