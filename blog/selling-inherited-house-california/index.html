Selling an Inherited House in California - Complete Guide

Published May 15, 2026 · Southern California
Back to All Articles

Inheriting a house in Southern California can feel overwhelming, especially when you're already dealing with the loss of a loved one. Between navigating California's probate process, understanding property tax implications, and deciding what to do with the property, there's a lot to consider. This guide walks you through everything you need to know about selling an inherited house in California.

Understanding California's Probate Process

When selling an inherited house in California, probate is often the first hurdle. Probate is the legal process that validates a will and transfers property ownership. In California, this process typically takes 9-18 months, though it can be longer for complex estates.

If the estate value exceeds $184,500, formal probate is usually required. However, California offers simplified procedures for smaller estates, including affidavits for personal property and expedited probate for estates under certain thresholds.

Probate vs. Trust Properties

Properties held in a living trust bypass probate entirely, allowing for much faster transfers. If your inherited property was in a trust, you may be able to sell it much sooner than properties going through probate court.

California Property Tax Implications

Understanding property taxes is crucial when selling an inherited house in California. Thanks to Proposition 19, which took effect in February 2021, the rules changed significantly for inherited properties.

Previously, parent-child transfers allowed you to keep the lower property tax assessment. Now, you can only maintain the parent's tax basis if:

  • The property is your primary residence
  • You move in within one year
  • The added value doesn't exceed $1 million over the factored base year value

For most inherited properties that won't become your primary residence, expect a reassessment to current market value, which in Orange County could mean significantly higher property taxes.

California-Specific Costs of Selling an Inherited Property

Selling an inherited house in California comes with several costs you should anticipate:

  • Probate fees: Attorney fees and executor fees are based on a percentage of the estate value (4% of the first $100,000, decreasing on higher amounts)
  • Back property taxes: Any unpaid taxes must be settled before sale
  • Transfer taxes: Vary by county; Orange County charges $0.55 per $500 of property value
  • Title insurance and escrow fees: Typically 1-2% of sale price in Southern California
  • Real estate commissions: Usually 5-6% if selling with an agent
  • Repairs and updates: Older inherited homes may need work to attract buyers

Dealing with Tenants in Inherited California Properties

If your inherited property has tenants, California's strong tenant protection laws come into play. You must honor existing leases and follow proper procedures for any changes. This includes providing proper notice for showings, respecting rent control ordinances in applicable cities, and following strict eviction procedures if necessary.

Many heirs find that selling to a cash buyer eliminates complications with tenant-occupied properties, as the buyer assumes responsibility for the tenancy.

Capital Gains Tax on Inherited California Properties

The good news: inherited properties receive a "step-up" in basis, meaning the property's value is reset to its fair market value at the date of death. If you sell relatively soon after inheriting, you'll likely owe little to no capital gains tax.

However, given Southern California's rapidly appreciating real estate market, holding the property for years before selling could result in substantial capital gains. The difference between the stepped-up basis and your sale price determines your taxable gain.

Steps to Selling Your Inherited California Home

Here's a practical roadmap for selling an inherited house in California:

  1. Determine ownership status: Was the property in a trust or does it require probate?
  2. Secure the property: Change locks, maintain insurance, and ensure it's protected
  3. Get a property valuation: Understand current Orange County market values
  4. Address outstanding debts: Identify any liens, mortgages, or back taxes
  5. Decide on repairs: Determine whether to fix issues or sell as-is
  6. Choose your selling method: Traditional sale or cash offer
  7. Complete required disclosures: California has strict disclosure requirements, even for inherited properties
  8. Close the sale: Work through escrow and transfer ownership
