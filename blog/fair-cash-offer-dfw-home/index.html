How to Get a Fair Cash Offer on Your DFW Home

Published September 28, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas

Selling your Dallas-Fort Worth home for cash can be a smart move when you need speed and certainty, but how do you know if you're getting a fair offer? Whether you're facing foreclosure, dealing with an inherited property, or simply want to avoid the traditional listing process, understanding what makes a cash offer fair is essential to protecting your investment in Texas's competitive real estate market.

The DFW real estate market has seen significant changes over the past few years, with home values fluctuating across different neighborhoods and cities. When you're considering a cash offer, you need to understand not just the current market conditions, but also how Texas-specific laws and costs factor into what constitutes a truly fair deal.

Understanding What 'Fair' Really Means in Cash Home Sales

A fair cash offer isn't necessarily the same as retail market value. Cash buyers typically offer 70-85% of a home's after-repair value, minus repair costs. This might sound low at first, but when you factor in what you're saving, the math often works in your favor.

In a traditional Texas home sale, you'll typically pay:

  • 6% in real estate agent commissions
  • 1-3% in closing costs
  • Title policy fees (which vary by county in DFW)
  • Prorated property taxes
  • Repair costs requested by buyers after inspection
  • Holding costs while the home sits on market (mortgage, utilities, insurance, HOA fees)

Texas is one of the few states where buyers typically pay for the owner's title policy, but as a seller, you'll still have costs. When you add up these expenses on a traditional sale, you're often looking at 8-12% of your home's value before you see a dime.

Texas-Specific Factors That Affect Your Cash Offer

Property Taxes and Homestead Exemptions

Texas property taxes are among the highest in the nation, with DFW counties ranging from 2-3% of assessed value annually. If you've been claiming a homestead exemption, you've been saving money, but this doesn't transfer to the next owner. Cash buyers factor in the full tax burden when evaluating your property.

Additionally, if you have any unpaid property taxes, they create a lien on your property that must be paid at closing. Reputable cash buyers will help you understand exactly what's owed and how it affects your net proceeds.

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, which means if you're married, both spouses typically need to sign off on the sale of a home, even if only one name is on the deed. This can complicate sales, especially in divorce situations. Cash buyers experienced with Texas law can navigate these situations smoothly, but it's something that affects the timeline and offer structure.

No State Income Tax Advantage

One benefit of selling in Texas is that you won't pay state income tax on your proceeds. While the federal capital gains exclusion still applies ($250,000 for individuals, $500,000 for married couples on primary residences), you won't face the additional state tax burden that sellers in California or New York deal with. This means more money stays in your pocket.

How to Evaluate If a Cash Offer Is Fair

Research Your Home's True Value

Start by understanding what your home would sell for in its current condition. Look at recent sales of comparable homes in your Plano, Frisco, Allen, Richardson, McKinney, or other DFW neighborhood. Pay attention to:

  • Homes that sold in the last 3-6 months
  • Properties in similar condition (not just renovated comps)
  • Square footage, lot size, and bedroom/bathroom count
  • School district ratings (a major factor in DFW pricing)

Websites like the Dallas Central Appraisal District or your county's appraisal district can provide valuable data, though remember that appraised value for tax purposes often differs from market value.

Calculate Your Net Proceeds from a Traditional Sale

Take your estimated market value and subtract all the costs you'd pay in a traditional sale. Don't forget to include:

  • Months of mortgage payments while listed
  • Utility bills during vacancy
  • HOA fees (especially relevant in master-planned communities throughout Frisco, Prosper, Celina, and Flower Mound)
  • Repairs requested during inspection periods
  • Pre-listing repairs your agent recommends
  • Staging and photography costs

This calculation gives you your true net proceeds from a traditional sale, which is the number you should compare against a cash offer.

Understand the Buyer's Perspective

Legitimate cash home buyers aren't trying to steal your property. They're running a business that involves risk, holding costs, renovation expenses, and eventual resale costs. When evaluating your home, they consider:

  • Repair costs (foundation issues are particularly common in DFW's clay soil)
  • Holding costs during renovation
  • Financing costs or opportunity costs of cash
  • Resale costs including commissions and holding time
  • Market risk if values decline during the flip period

A fair cash offer accounts for these factors while still providing you with a reasonable amount for your property.

Red Flags: How to Spot Unfair Cash Offers

Not all cash buyers operate with integrity. Watch out for these warning signs:

Lowball Offers with No Explanation

A legitimate buyer will explain their offer and break down their calculations. If someone offers you 40-50% of your home's value without justifying the number, be suspicious. In the current DFW market, fair offers typically range from 70-85% of after-repair value.

Pressure Tactics and Limited-Time Offers

Reputable companies give you time to consider their offer and consult with family, attorneys, or financial advisors. High-pressure sales tactics suggesting you must decide immediately are major red flags.

Fees or Upfront Costs

You should never pay fees to receive a cash offer or to sell your home to a cash buyer. If someone asks for processing fees, evaluation fees, or any upfront payment, walk away. This is not standard practice in Texas.

No Proof of Funds

Before committing to a cash sale, verify the buyer actually has the money. Legitimate buyers will provide proof of funds when requested. This is especially important given Texas's fast foreclosure timeline—if you're selling to stop foreclosure, you can't afford a buyer who can't close quickly.

Special Situations That Affect Fair Offer Calculations

Foundation Issues in DFW Homes

The expansive clay soil throughout Dallas, Fort Worth, Plano, and surrounding areas causes foundation problems in many homes. Foundation repairs can cost $5,000 to $50,000 or more depending on severity. Cash buyers will reduce their offer to account for these repairs, but this is actually fair—a traditional buyer would either demand you fix it or negotiate a similar price reduction.

HOA Violations and Fees

Texas HOAs have significant power, and many DFW communities have strict rules. If you have outstanding HOA fees or violations, these create liens that must be resolved at closing. Make sure any cash offer accounts for these costs appropriately.

Texas Non-Judicial Foreclosure Timeline

Texas allows non-judicial foreclosures, which can happen quickly—sometimes in as little as 27 days from the first notice of default. If you're selling to avoid foreclosure, time is critical. A cash buyer who can close in 7-14 days provides value beyond just the dollar amount by saving your credit and potentially your equity.

Questions to Ask Before Accepting Any Cash Offer

Before signing a purchase agreement with any cash buyer in DFW, ask:

  • Can you explain how you calculated this offer?
  • What comparable sales did you use?
  • What repair costs are you estimating?
  • Are there any fees I'll need to pay?
  • What's your timeline from contract to closing?
  • Are there any contingencies in your offer?
  • Can you provide proof of funds?
  • Can you provide references from recent sellers?
  • What happens if I need to close faster or slower than your standard timeline?

A legitimate cash home buyer will answer all these questions clearly and without hesitation.
