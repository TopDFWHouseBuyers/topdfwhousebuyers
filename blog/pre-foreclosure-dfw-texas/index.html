Pre-Foreclosure in DFW - What It Means for Homeowners

Published August 19, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

If you've fallen behind on your mortgage payments in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, you might be facing pre-foreclosure—a stressful situation that leaves many homeowners feeling overwhelmed and uncertain about their options. Pre-foreclosure is the period between when your lender initiates the foreclosure process and when your home is actually sold at auction, and understanding what this means under Texas law can help you make informed decisions about your property. The good news is that during this window, you still have rights and options to protect yourself and potentially save your home or your equity.

What Is Pre-Foreclosure in Texas?

Pre-foreclosure begins when you've missed several mortgage payments—typically three to six months—and your lender has officially started the foreclosure process by filing a Notice of Default. In Texas, this is a critical period because it's your last opportunity to take action before losing your home at a foreclosure auction.

Unlike judicial foreclosure states where the process goes through court, Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state. This means lenders can foreclose relatively quickly without going to court, as long as they follow the procedures outlined in the Texas Property Code. The foreclosure clause in most Texas mortgages includes a 'power of sale' provision that allows this expedited process.

The Texas Foreclosure Timeline

Understanding the timeline is crucial for DFW homeowners facing pre-foreclosure:

  • Day 1-90: You miss mortgage payments, and your lender contacts you about the delinquency
  • Day 90-120: The lender files a Notice of Default and sends you official foreclosure notices
  • Day 120+: At least 21 days before the foreclosure sale, you must receive written notice by certified mail
  • Sale Date: Foreclosure auctions in Texas happen on the first Tuesday of each month at the county courthouse

This timeline can vary, but Texas law requires lenders to provide at least 20 days' notice before the foreclosure sale. The entire process from first missed payment to auction can take as little as four to six months in Texas, which is considerably faster than many other states.

Your Rights as a Texas Homeowner in Pre-Foreclosure

Texas offers some unique protections for homeowners, and knowing your rights can make a significant difference during pre-foreclosure.

Texas Homestead Exemption

Texas has one of the strongest homestead exemptions in the country. This constitutional protection means that while your mortgage lender can foreclose on your home for non-payment, most other creditors cannot force the sale of your primary residence to satisfy debts. The homestead exemption protects unlimited home value on up to 10 acres in urban areas (which includes most of DFW) or up to 100 acres for single persons and 200 acres for families in rural areas.

However, it's important to understand that the homestead exemption doesn't prevent foreclosure by your mortgage lender—it only protects against other creditors.

No Deficiency Judgments in Many Cases

Texas Property Code Section 51.003 provides important protection: if your lender uses the non-judicial foreclosure process and the property is your homestead, they generally cannot pursue you for a deficiency judgment. This means if your home sells at auction for less than what you owe, you typically won't be personally liable for the difference on a home equity loan or reverse mortgage.

Right to Reinstate

Until the foreclosure sale actually occurs, you have the right to reinstate your loan by paying all past-due amounts, late fees, and foreclosure costs. This stops the foreclosure process and brings your mortgage current.

Your Options During Pre-Foreclosure in Dallas-Fort Worth

The pre-foreclosure period is your window of opportunity. Here are the options available to DFW homeowners:

Loan Modification or Forbearance

Contact your lender immediately to discuss restructuring your loan. Many lenders would prefer to modify your loan terms rather than foreclose. Options might include extending the loan term, reducing the interest rate temporarily, or adding missed payments to the end of the loan.

Repayment Plan

If your financial hardship was temporary, you might arrange a repayment plan that allows you to catch up on missed payments over several months while continuing to make current payments.

Refinance

If you have sufficient equity and your credit hasn't been too damaged, refinancing might be possible. However, this can be challenging once you're already in pre-foreclosure, and closing costs can be substantial in Texas—typically 2-5% of the loan amount.

Short Sale

With your lender's approval, you can sell your home for less than what you owe. This process can take several months and requires extensive documentation, but it's less damaging to your credit than foreclosure.

The Financial Impact of Pre-Foreclosure in Texas

Credit Score Damage

Missing mortgage payments damages your credit score significantly—typically 100-150 points or more. Pre-foreclosure status can appear on your credit report, and if the foreclosure completes, the impact lasts seven years from the date of the first missed payment.

Texas Foreclosure Costs

Foreclosure isn't free for lenders, and they typically add these costs to what you owe:

  • Attorney fees: $2,000-$5,000 or more
  • Trustee fees and posting costs: $500-$1,500
  • Property preservation costs: Varies widely
  • Recording fees and documentation: $200-$500

These costs reduce any equity you might recover if the home sells for more than you owe.

Special Considerations for DFW Homeowners

Property Taxes in Texas

Texas has no state income tax, which is great, but the state relies heavily on property taxes for revenue. DFW property tax rates typically range from 2-3% of your home's assessed value annually. If you're behind on property taxes, this creates an additional lien on your property that must be satisfied before you can sell.

Property tax liens take priority over mortgage liens in Texas, and counties can foreclose for unpaid property taxes. If you're in pre-foreclosure and also behind on property taxes, your situation requires immediate attention.

HOA Considerations

Many DFW communities—especially newer developments in Frisco, Prosper, Celina, and The Colony—have homeowners associations. If you're behind on HOA dues, the association can file a lien against your property and eventually foreclose. Unpaid HOA dues must be addressed when selling your home.

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, meaning debts incurred during marriage are typically shared by both spouses. If you're married and facing pre-foreclosure, both you and your spouse are generally responsible for the mortgage debt, even if only one name is on the loan, if the home was purchased during the marriage.
