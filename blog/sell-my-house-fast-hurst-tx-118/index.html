Texas-Specific Considerations for Hurst Rental Property Owners

Selling a rental property in Texas involves unique considerations that differ from selling your primary residence. Understanding these factors helps you make informed decisions about your Hurst investment property.

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, meaning if you're married, your spouse typically has equal ownership interest in rental properties acquired during marriage. Both spouses will generally need to sign off on any sale, making clear communication essential when deciding to sell your Hurst rental.

Capital Gains and Tax Advantages

One significant advantage of selling property in Texas is the absence of state income tax. While you'll still owe federal capital gains taxes on your rental property sale, you won't face additional state-level taxation. This can result in substantial savings compared to landlords in states like California or New York. However, you'll want to consult with a tax professional about depreciation recapture and potential 1031 exchange options.

Homestead Exemption Doesn't Apply

Unlike your primary residence, rental properties don't qualify for Texas homestead exemption protections. This means your Hurst investment property doesn't receive the same property tax benefits or creditor protections as an owner-occupied home—another reason many landlords decide the investment isn't worth maintaining.

Tenant Rights and Eviction Timeline

If you're selling with tenants in place, Texas law provides specific guidelines. While Texas eviction processes are generally landlord-friendly compared to other states, you still face a timeline of 3-4 weeks minimum for uncontested evictions. Many cash buyers, including Top DFW House Buyers, will purchase properties with tenants in place, allowing you to avoid the eviction process entirely.
