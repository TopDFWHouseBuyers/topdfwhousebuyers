How to Sell Your Texas Home When Relocating

Published November 09, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Relocating can be both exciting and overwhelming, especially when you need to sell your Dallas-Fort Worth home quickly. Whether you're moving for a job opportunity, family reasons, or lifestyle change, coordinating your home sale with your move timeline adds another layer of complexity to an already stressful situation.

Selling a home in Texas while managing a relocation requires careful planning and understanding of your options. The good news is that the DFW real estate market offers several pathways to a successful sale, even when you're working against the clock.

Understanding Your Timeline When Relocating

Your relocation timeline will significantly impact your selling strategy. If you have several months before your move, a traditional listing might work well. However, many relocating homeowners face tighter deadlines that require alternative solutions.

In the Dallas-Fort Worth area, traditional home sales typically take 30-60 days from listing to closing, though this varies by neighborhood and market conditions. Cities like Plano, Frisco, and Southlake often see faster sales due to high demand, while other areas might take longer.

Factors Affecting Your Selling Timeline

  • Your employer's relocation deadline
  • School enrollment dates if you have children
  • Start date at your new job
  • Current market conditions in your specific DFW neighborhood
  • Your home's condition and whether it needs repairs
  • Seasonal market fluctuations in Texas

Texas-Specific Considerations for Relocating Sellers

Texas has unique laws and market conditions that affect home sales, especially when you're relocating out of state or to another part of Texas.

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, meaning if you're married, both spouses typically have equal ownership rights to property acquired during the marriage. When selling while relocating, both spouses must sign the sales documents, even if only one name is on the title. This is particularly important if one spouse is already relocated while the other handles the sale.

Homestead Exemption Benefits

If you've claimed a homestead exemption on your Texas property, you've enjoyed significant property tax savings. When relocating, you'll lose this exemption, which is one reason many homeowners choose to sell rather than rent out their primary residence. The exemption cannot be transferred to a rental property.

No State Income Tax Advantage

One benefit of selling in Texas is the absence of state income tax on your sale proceeds. If you're relocating out of state, understanding the tax implications in your new location becomes important for financial planning.

Challenges of Selling While Relocating

Relocating homeowners face unique obstacles that make traditional home sales more difficult.

Managing Showings from a Distance

Once you've moved, coordinating showings, inspections, and repairs becomes challenging. You may need to hire property managers or rely on real estate agents to handle everything remotely, which can delay the process and add costs.

Carrying Two Housing Payments

Many relocating families face the burden of paying for housing in their new location while still covering the mortgage, property taxes, HOA fees, and utilities on their DFW home. In areas like Frisco, Southlake, or Colleyville where HOA fees can be substantial, these costs add up quickly.

Home Preparation and Repairs

Traditional buyers often request repairs after inspections. When you're already relocated, managing contractors and ensuring quality work from hundreds or thousands of miles away creates additional stress and expense.

Your Options for Selling When Relocating

Understanding your options helps you choose the best path based on your specific timeline and circumstances.

Traditional Listing with a Real Estate Agent

This approach works well if you have 2-4 months before relocating. An experienced DFW agent can handle marketing, showings, and negotiations. However, you'll need to prepare your home for market, remain flexible for showings, and potentially make repairs after inspections.

Commission costs in Texas typically range from 5-6% of the sale price, plus you'll cover closing costs of 1-3%. On a $300,000 home in Richardson or Garland, that's $15,000-$18,000 in commissions alone.

Rent Your Home

Some relocating homeowners consider renting their Texas property. However, this requires becoming a long-distance landlord, managing tenants, maintenance issues, and potential vacancy periods. You'll also lose your homestead exemption, and property taxes will increase accordingly.

Steps to Sell Your Home While Relocating

Follow these steps to streamline your sale and reduce relocation stress.

1. Assess Your Timeline

Determine your must-move date and work backward. Be realistic about how much time you have for home preparation, marketing, and closing.

2. Evaluate Your Home's Condition

Honestly assess whether your home is market-ready or needs work. Consider repair costs, time investment, and whether you can manage projects while relocating.

3. Research Your Options

Get multiple perspectives. Talk to real estate agents about market value and timing, and contact cash buyers for fast-sale options. Having multiple offers helps you make an informed decision.

4. Gather Important Documents

Collect your property deed, mortgage information, HOA documents, tax statements, and any warranties or repair records. Having these ready speeds up the selling process regardless of which option you choose.

5. Understand Your Costs

Calculate your bottom line for each selling option. Include commissions, closing costs, repair expenses, carrying costs, and moving expenses in your decision.

Texas Market Conditions to Consider

The DFW market has remained strong in recent years, but conditions vary by city and neighborhood. Plano, Frisco, and McKinney have seen consistent appreciation, while other areas experience more fluctuation.

Timing your sale with Texas market seasons can impact results. Spring and fall typically see the most buyer activity, while summer and winter can slow down. However, when relocating, your timeline may not allow you to wait for optimal market conditions—another reason cash sales appeal to many relocating homeowners.

Avoiding Common Relocation Selling Mistakes

Don't make these common errors that cost relocating sellers time and money.

Overpricing Your Home

Emotional attachment and relocation expenses tempt some sellers to overprice. In competitive DFW markets like Coppell or Southlake, overpriced homes sit on the market and eventually sell for less than properly priced properties.

Neglecting Curb Appeal

Even if you're already relocated, first impressions matter for traditional buyers. Ensure lawns are maintained and the exterior is presentable if pursuing a traditional sale.

Underestimating Costs

Factor in all expenses: mortgage payments until closing, utilities, HOA fees, property taxes, insurance, lawn care, and potential repair costs. In Texas, property taxes are substantial, and carrying costs can quickly erode your equity.

Not Considering Cash Offers

Many homeowners assume cash offers will be too low without exploring the option. When you factor in commission savings, repair costs, carrying costs, and the value of certainty and speed, cash offers often net more than expected—especially when relocating.

Special Situations for Relocating Sellers

Military Relocations

DFW is home to significant military populations near Fort Worth and surrounding areas. Military families often face short-notice relocations with inflexible timelines. The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act provides some protections, but selling quickly often provides the cleanest solution.

Job Relocations

Corporate relocations to or from DFW are common given the area's business growth. Some employers offer relocation assistance that may include home-buying programs. Understand what your employer offers and how it affects your timeline and selling options.

Inherited Properties

If you've inherited a DFW property while living elsewhere, selling quickly often makes sense to avoid ongoing expenses and management headaches. Cash buyers can purchase inherited homes regardless of condition, even with multiple heirs involved.
