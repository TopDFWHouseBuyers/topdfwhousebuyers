How Fast Can You Sell a House in DFW?

Published May 23, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

If you need to sell your house quickly in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, you are probably wondering exactly how long the process will take. The answer depends on your selling method, market conditions, and how much flexibility you have with price and terms.

Traditional Sale Timeline in DFW: 60-90 Days Minimum

When you list with a real estate agent in the DFW market, the typical timeline runs between 60 to 90 days, though it can stretch much longer. First, you will spend 1-2 weeks preparing your home—cleaning, making repairs, and staging. Then your agent will list the property and schedule showings, which can take anywhere from a few days to several months depending on market conditions and your asking price.

Once you receive an offer and go under contract, the buyer will typically need 30-45 days for financing approval, home inspections, and appraisals. In Texas, buyers have the right to conduct inspections during the option period (usually 7-10 days), during which they can request repairs or even back out of the deal. If the appraisal comes in low or financing falls through, you may be back to square one.

After closing is scheduled, you will need to account for the title company to complete their work, which usually takes another 1-2 weeks. Throughout this process, you will be paying mortgage payments, utilities, insurance, and maintenance costs while the house sits on the market.

What Affects How Quickly Your DFW Home Sells

Several factors impact your timeline in the Dallas-Fort Worth market. Location matters significantly—homes in Plano, Frisco, Southlake, and other desirable areas typically sell faster than properties in less sought-after neighborhoods. Property condition plays a major role too. Homes needing significant repairs or updates often sit on the market longer, as most traditional buyers are looking for move-in-ready properties.

Your asking price is critical. Overpriced homes can languish for months, even in hot markets like McKinney, Allen, or Colleyville. Market conditions also matter—while DFW has remained relatively strong, seasonal fluctuations and economic factors can slow things down. Finally, your buyer matters. Cash buyers close much faster than those requiring financing, which involves lender underwriting, appraisals, and potential delays.

Under Texas law, sellers must complete a Seller Disclosure Notice, and any title issues must be resolved before closing, which can add time to your transaction.
