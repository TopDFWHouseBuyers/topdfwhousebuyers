Selling a House As-Is in Texas - What Sellers Need to Know

Published May 27, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Selling a house as-is in Texas means offering your property in its current condition without making repairs or improvements. If you are facing foreclosure, dealing with an inherited property, or simply do not want to invest time and money into fixes, understanding the as-is sale process can save you stress and money.

What Does Selling As-Is Actually Mean in Texas?

When you sell a house as-is in Texas, you are telling buyers they will receive the property in its present condition. This does not mean you can hide problems or avoid disclosures. Under Texas Property Code Section 5.008, sellers must still complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice for most residential transactions, revealing known defects and issues.

The key difference is that you are not obligated to make repairs based on inspection findings. Buyers purchase the home knowing they will handle any needed work themselves. This approach works well for properties needing significant repairs, inherited homes, or situations where sellers need to close quickly.

Texas Disclosure Requirements for As-Is Sales

Texas law requires sellers to provide a Seller's Disclosure of Property Condition form for most residential sales, even in as-is transactions. You must disclose known issues with the foundation, roof, plumbing, electrical systems, and other major components. Failing to disclose known defects can lead to legal liability after closing.

There are exceptions to disclosure requirements, including foreclosures, estate sales from court-appointed administrators, and sales between co-owners. Cash buyers and real estate investors often purchase as-is properties without requiring extensive disclosures, making the process simpler for sellers in the Dallas-Fort Worth area.

Costs Associated with As-Is Sales in Texas

While you avoid repair costs when selling as-is, other expenses still apply. Standard closing costs in Texas typically include title insurance, escrow fees, and property taxes prorated to the closing date. Sellers usually pay the owner's title policy, which costs around 0.9% of the sale price in the DFW area.

If you work with a traditional real estate agent, expect to pay 5-6% in commission fees. However, selling to a cash buyer eliminates agent commissions and often covers your closing costs entirely, putting more money in your pocket despite selling below market value.

When Selling As-Is Makes Sense

Several situations make as-is sales attractive for Texas homeowners. If your property needs repairs exceeding $15,000-$20,000, you might not recoup those costs in a traditional sale. Inherited properties in poor condition, homes facing foreclosure, or properties with title issues are ideal for as-is sales.

Time constraints also matter. Traditional sales in the DFW market average 30-60 days from listing to closing, not including time for repairs and staging. As-is cash sales can close in as little as 7-10 days, helping sellers who need to relocate quickly or avoid foreclosure.

Working with Cash Buyers vs. Traditional Buyers

Traditional buyers using financing often struggle to purchase as-is properties because lenders require homes to meet minimum condition standards. FHA and VA loans have strict property requirements that distressed homes rarely meet without repairs.

Cash buyers and real estate investors specialize in as-is purchases throughout Plano, Frisco, Allen, Richardson, McKinney, Arlington, and all DFW cities. They make offers based on current condition, handle all repairs after purchase, and close quickly without financing contingencies or inspection issues.

The As-Is Sale Process in Texas

Start by gathering documentation about your property, including the deed, mortgage information, and any known repair estimates. Complete the Seller's Disclosure form honestly, noting all known issues. Contact cash buyers for offers, which typically arrive within 24-48 hours.

Once you accept an offer, the buyer handles title work and schedules closing. Texas is an attorney state, meaning lawyers typically handle closings, though title companies manage most transactions in the DFW area. You will sign the deed and closing documents, receive your payment, and hand over the keys—often completing everything within two weeks.

Avoiding As-Is Sale Scams

Protect yourself by working with licensed, reputable buyers. Verify the buyer's credentials and check reviews from past sellers. Never pay upfront fees to sell your home, and be cautious of buyers who pressure you to sign quickly without reading documents.
