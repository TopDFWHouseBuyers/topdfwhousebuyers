Selling a House As-Is in Texas - What Sellers Need to Know

Published September 21, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Selling a house as-is in Texas means you're offering your property in its current condition, without making repairs or improvements before closing. For Dallas-Fort Worth homeowners facing financial challenges, inherited properties, or homes needing extensive repairs, this option can provide a faster, less stressful path to selling. Understanding Texas-specific laws and disclosure requirements will help you navigate this process confidently.

What Does 'As-Is' Really Mean in Texas?

When you sell a house as-is in Texas, you're telling buyers that you won't be making repairs or improvements before closing. However, this doesn't mean you can hide problems or skip required disclosures. Texas law still requires sellers to be honest about their property's condition.

Many DFW homeowners mistakenly believe that selling as-is means they have no legal obligations. The truth is more nuanced. You must still comply with Texas Property Code requirements, particularly regarding seller disclosures, which we'll discuss in detail below.

Texas Seller Disclosure Requirements

Texas is one of the most seller-friendly states when it comes to disclosures, but you still have legal obligations. The Texas Property Code requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice for most residential transactions.

What You Must Disclose

Even when selling as-is, Texas law requires you to disclose known material defects that could affect the property's value or desirability. This includes:

  • Foundation problems or structural issues
  • Roof leaks or damage
  • Plumbing or electrical system defects
  • Previous flooding or water damage
  • Termite or pest infestations
  • HOA violations or pending assessments
  • Lead-based paint (for homes built before 1978)

In the Dallas-Fort Worth area, foundation issues are particularly important to disclose due to our expansive clay soil. Richardson, Plano, and Frisco homeowners often deal with foundation settlement, and failing to disclose known problems can lead to legal liability even in an as-is sale.

Exemptions from Disclosure

Texas law provides certain exemptions from the Seller's Disclosure Notice requirement, including transfers to spouse or family members, court-ordered sales, foreclosures, and sales to cash buyers who waive the disclosure in writing. Many professional cash home buyers in the DFW area will waive this requirement, simplifying your transaction.

Costs of Selling As-Is in Texas

One of the biggest advantages of selling as-is in Texas is avoiding repair costs, but you'll still face some expenses. Understanding these costs helps you calculate your net proceeds.

Typical Selling Costs in the DFW Market

  • Real estate commissions: Traditional sales typically cost 5-6% in agent commissions. On a $300,000 home in Frisco or Allen, that's $15,000-$18,000.
  • Title and closing costs: In Texas, sellers typically pay for the owner's title policy, which costs around $1,000-$2,000 depending on the sale price.
  • HOA fees: Many DFW communities, especially in Plano, McKinney, and Southlake, have HOAs. You'll need to pay any outstanding dues and may owe transfer fees.
  • Pro-rated property taxes: Texas has relatively high property taxes (no state income tax means municipalities rely heavily on property tax). You'll owe taxes up to closing.
  • Transfer fees: Some cities in the Dallas-Fort Worth area charge transfer fees or require inspections.

The good news? Texas has no state income tax, so you won't face state capital gains taxes on your sale proceeds. Federal capital gains taxes may apply, but the homestead exemption (discussed below) can help.

Cost Savings of As-Is Sales

Selling as-is saves you from repair costs that can quickly accumulate. A foundation repair in Dallas or Fort Worth can cost $5,000-$20,000 or more. Roof replacements in our hail-prone area run $8,000-$15,000. Kitchen and bathroom renovations can exceed $25,000. By selling as-is, you avoid these expenses entirely.

Texas-Specific Legal Considerations

Community Property Laws

Texas is a community property state, which affects how you sell your home. If you're married, your spouse generally has ownership rights in your home even if only your name is on the deed. Both spouses typically must sign the deed to sell the property, and both should be involved in disclosure decisions.

If you're going through a divorce in Plano, Arlington, or anywhere in DFW, selling as-is can simplify the division of assets. Cash buyers can often close quickly, helping you move forward faster.

Texas Homestead Exemption Benefits

Texas offers generous homestead protections that benefit sellers. If the home is your primary residence, you may qualify for substantial capital gains tax exclusions: up to $250,000 for single filers and $500,000 for married couples filing jointly. This means most DFW homeowners pay zero federal capital gains tax when selling their primary residence.

The homestead exemption also protects your home from most creditors while you own it, though this protection ends at sale. If you're facing judgments or liens, selling as-is to a cash buyer may help you resolve these issues quickly.

Texas Foreclosure Timeline and As-Is Sales

Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state with one of the fastest foreclosure processes in the nation. From the first missed payment to foreclosure sale can be as short as 4-5 months. If you're behind on payments in Garland, Grand Prairie, or any DFW city, selling as-is can help you avoid foreclosure.

The Texas foreclosure process requires a 20-day notice before the sale date. Working with a cash buyer who can close in 7-14 days might save your credit and help you walk away with equity instead of facing foreclosure.

Your Options for Selling As-Is in the DFW Area

Traditional Listing

You can list your as-is home with a real estate agent on the MLS. This approach may get you the highest price, but it comes with challenges. As-is homes typically attract fewer buyers, sit on the market longer, and receive lower offers than move-in-ready properties. You'll still pay full commissions (5-6%), and buyers may request inspections that could lead to further price negotiations or deals falling through.

In competitive DFW markets like Prosper, Celina, or North Richland Hills, as-is listings may struggle if other homes show better. You'll also need to maintain the property, handle showings, and wait 30-60 days for financing to close (if a buyer even qualifies).

Common Concerns About As-Is Sales in Texas

Will I Get a Fair Price?

As-is homes typically sell for less than renovated properties, but you save on repair costs, commissions, and holding costs. When you factor in what you'd spend fixing up a home in Hurst, Bedford, or Coppell, as-is sales often net similar or better proceeds with far less hassle.

What About HOA Issues?

Many DFW neighborhoods have active HOAs with strict rules. If you have HOA violations or unpaid assessments, disclose these to potential buyers. Cash buyers typically purchase homes even with HOA issues, while traditional buyers may walk away.

Can I Sell with Code Violations?

Yes, you can sell a house with code violations in Texas, but you must disclose them. Some cities in the DFW area have stricter enforcement than others. Cash buyers regularly purchase homes with violation notices, liens, or required repairs.

What If I Have Equity But No Cash for Repairs?

This is one of the most common situations for DFW homeowners. You have equity built up in your Arlington, Euless, or McKinney home, but you can't access it without selling. As-is sales let you convert that equity to cash without spending money upfront on repairs.
