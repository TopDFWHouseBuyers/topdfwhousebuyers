What Happens at a Cash Home Sale Closing in Texas?

Published October 02, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

If you're selling your home for cash in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, you might be wondering what actually happens at closing. The good news is that cash closings in Texas are typically faster and simpler than traditional financed sales, often wrapping up in as little as 7-14 days. Let's walk through exactly what you can expect during a cash home sale closing in Texas so you feel confident and prepared when the big day arrives.

Understanding the Cash Closing Process in Texas

A cash home sale closing in Texas differs significantly from traditional closings because there's no lender involved. Without the need for mortgage underwriting, appraisals for financing purposes, or bank-required repairs, the process moves much faster and with fewer contingencies.

In Texas, real estate closings are typically handled by title companies rather than attorneys (though you can hire an attorney if you prefer). The title company acts as a neutral third party to ensure all documents are properly executed and funds are correctly distributed.

This speed is particularly valuable if you're facing foreclosure in Texas. Unlike judicial foreclosure states, Texas follows a non-judicial foreclosure process that can move quickly—sometimes in as little as 41 days from the first notice of default. A fast cash closing can help you avoid foreclosure and protect your credit.

Before Closing Day: What Happens Behind the Scenes

Several important steps occur between accepting an offer and sitting down at the closing table.

Title Search and Title Insurance

The title company will conduct a thorough title search to ensure you have clear ownership and can legally sell the property. They'll check for liens, judgments, unpaid property taxes, or other encumbrances that need to be addressed.

In Texas, it's customary for the seller to provide an owner's title insurance policy to the buyer. The cost typically ranges from $1,000 to $3,000 depending on your home's sale price, and this is usually the seller's largest closing cost in a cash transaction.

HOA Documentation

If your Dallas-Fort Worth property is in a homeowners association—common in communities throughout Plano, Frisco, McKinney, Southlake, and other DFW cities—the title company will request HOA documentation. This includes a resale certificate showing your account status, outstanding dues, and any violations.

Texas law requires HOA resale certificates to be provided to buyers. Any unpaid HOA dues or fees will typically be paid from your proceeds at closing.

Preparing Required Disclosures

Texas law requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice for most residential transactions. However, many cash buyers, especially investors and companies like Top DFW House Buyers, purchase properties 'as-is' and may not require this disclosure, simplifying your preparation.

What Happens on Closing Day

Closing day for a cash sale in Texas is typically straightforward and takes 30-60 minutes.

Reviewing and Signing Documents

You'll meet at the title company office (or they may come to you—mobile closings are common in DFW). The closing agent will guide you through each document, explaining what you're signing.

Key documents include:

  • Deed: Transfers ownership from you to the buyer. In Texas, this is typically a Special Warranty Deed for investor purchases.
  • Settlement Statement: Itemizes all debits and credits, showing exactly how much money you'll receive.
  • Affidavits: You may sign affidavits confirming your identity, marital status, and that you haven't incurred recent liens.
  • FIRPTA: A federal form certifying you're a U.S. citizen or resident (to avoid withholding requirements).

Texas Community Property Considerations

Texas is a community property state, which affects how you close. If you're married, both spouses typically must sign the deed even if only one spouse is on the title. This protects homestead rights under Texas law.

If you're divorced, you may need to provide your divorce decree. If your spouse is deceased, the title company will need documentation of their passing and may require an affidavit of heirship depending on how title was held.

Receiving Your Proceeds

Once all documents are signed, the closing agent will verify everything is in order. In a cash transaction, funds are already available (unlike financed deals where you wait for the lender to wire money).

You'll typically receive your proceeds via:

  • Wire transfer (most common—usually available same day or next business day)
  • Cashier's check (can be picked up or mailed)

Many DFW sellers prefer wire transfers for speed and security, though there's usually a small wire fee ($25-50).

Texas-Specific Closing Costs for Cash Sales

One advantage of Texas is that we have no state income tax, so you won't owe state capital gains taxes on your sale. You may owe federal capital gains, but the IRS homestead exemption allows you to exclude up to $250,000 in gains ($500,000 for married couples) if you've lived in the home as your primary residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years.

What You'll Typically Pay

In a cash sale to a buyer like Top DFW House Buyers, seller closing costs are usually minimal:

  • Owner's title insurance: $1,000-$3,000 (your largest cost)
  • HOA fees: Any unpaid dues, plus sometimes a transfer fee ($100-$500)
  • Pro-rated property taxes: You'll pay taxes up to the closing date
  • Recording fees: Usually under $100

What you typically DON'T pay in a cash sale:

  • Real estate agent commissions (5-6% in traditional sales)
  • Buyer's closing costs or loan-related fees
  • Repairs or inspection-related credits

Common Questions About Cash Closings in Texas

Do I need an attorney for a cash closing in Texas?

Texas doesn't require attorneys for real estate closings. Title companies handle most transactions, and they're specifically licensed and insured for this purpose. However, you're always welcome to hire an attorney if you prefer, especially for complex situations.

What if there's a lien on my property?

Liens don't necessarily prevent you from selling. Most liens (mortgages, HELOCs, judgment liens, tax liens) are paid from your proceeds at closing. The title company calculates payoffs and ensures all liens are satisfied so the buyer receives clear title.

If you owe more than the home is worth, that's a different situation requiring either a short sale or bringing money to closing, but a qualified cash buyer can help you explore options.

Can I close remotely if I've already moved?

Yes! Remote closings are common in Texas. The title company can overnight documents to you, or arrange for a mobile notary in your new location. Some Texas title companies now offer remote online notarization (RON) for even more convenience.

What about my homestead exemption?

Your Texas homestead exemption for property tax purposes automatically ends when you sell. You don't need to do anything—the county appraisal district is notified of the sale and removes the exemption. If you're buying another home in Texas, remember to file for homestead exemption at your new property.

After Closing: What Happens Next

Once you've signed all documents and closing is complete, the title company will record the deed with the county clerk's office in your DFW county (Dallas County, Tarrant County, Collin County, Denton County, etc.). Recording typically happens within 1-3 business days.

You'll receive copies of all your closing documents for your records. Keep these in a safe place—you'll need them for tax purposes and future reference.

Keys and Possession

In Texas, possession typically transfers at closing unless otherwise specified in your contract. This means you should plan to have the property completely vacated, cleaned out, and keys available by the closing date. Many cash buyers offer flexibility on possession dates if you need a few extra days to move.
