Is Now a Good Time to Sell Your DFW Home in 2026?

Published July 15, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

If you are considering selling your home in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, you are probably wondering whether 2026 is the right time to make your move. With shifting interest rates, evolving buyer demand, and local market dynamics, the decision can feel overwhelming.

Understanding the 2026 DFW Real Estate Market

The Dallas-Fort Worth real estate market continues to show resilience in 2026, though conditions have shifted from the frenzied pace of previous years. The DFW metroplex remains one of the fastest-growing regions in the country, with companies relocating here and population growth driving consistent housing demand. Cities like Plano, Frisco, McKinney, and Southlake continue attracting buyers seeking quality schools and job opportunities.

Current inventory levels have normalized compared to the severe shortages of recent years, giving sellers a more balanced market. While homes may not sell within hours like they did during the pandemic boom, well-priced properties in desirable areas still move quickly. The key is understanding your specific neighborhood and property condition.

Interest Rates and Buyer Activity in Texas

Mortgage interest rates in 2026 have stabilized but remain higher than the historic lows of 2020-2021. This has changed buyer behavior somewhat, making affordability a bigger concern. However, the strong Texas economy and lack of state income tax continue making the DFW area attractive to out-of-state buyers and local move-up purchasers.

For sellers, this means pricing strategy matters more than ever. Overpriced homes sit on the market longer, while competitively priced properties still generate multiple offers. If your home needs significant repairs or updates, you may face challenges attracting traditional financed buyers who struggle with appraisal issues.

Texas-Specific Selling Costs to Consider

When evaluating if 2026 is a good time to sell your DFW home, understand the costs involved. Texas sellers typically pay 5-6% in realtor commissions, plus closing costs that can add another 1-3% of the sale price. Title insurance, required in Texas, protects buyers and lenders but adds to your expense. If you have an outstanding mortgage, factor in payoff amounts and potential prepayment penalties.

Property taxes in areas like Collin County and Tarrant County have increased substantially, making home ownership more expensive for buyers. This affects how much buyers can afford, potentially impacting your sale price. Additionally, if you purchased your home recently, you may have minimal equity after paying selling costs through traditional methods.

Should You Wait or Sell Now?

Timing the market perfectly is nearly impossible, but certain situations make 2026 a good time to sell your DFW home regardless of broader trends. If you are facing foreclosure, dealing with divorce, inherited a property you cannot maintain, or need to relocate for work, waiting may cost you more than selling now.

Life circumstances often matter more than market conditions. A home requiring extensive repairs will only deteriorate further with time, potentially costing you more money. If your property is becoming a financial burden through maintenance, insurance, or tax expenses, selling sooner rather than later makes sense.
