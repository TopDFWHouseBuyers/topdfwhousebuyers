Selling a House With Foundation Problems in Texas

Published October 19, 2026 · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas
Back to All Articles

Foundation problems are among the most stressful issues a Dallas-Fort Worth homeowner can face. Whether you've noticed cracked walls, uneven floors, or doors that won't close properly, selling a house with foundation problems in Texas comes with unique challenges—but it's absolutely possible with the right approach.

The expansive clay soil throughout the DFW Metroplex creates the perfect storm for foundation issues. One season brings drought that causes soil to contract, the next brings heavy rain that makes it expand. This constant movement puts tremendous stress on foundations, making foundation problems incredibly common across Plano, Frisco, Arlington, and surrounding cities.

Understanding Foundation Repair Costs in Texas

Before deciding how to sell your home, it's important to understand what you're dealing with financially. Foundation repair costs in the Dallas-Fort Worth area typically range from $3,500 for minor pier and beam adjustments to $35,000 or more for extensive slab foundation repairs requiring numerous piers.

Several factors affect your repair costs:

  • Type of foundation (slab, pier and beam, or basement)
  • Severity and extent of the damage
  • Accessibility of the affected areas
  • Soil conditions on your property
  • Square footage of your home

Most foundation repair companies in Texas offer free inspections, so you can get a detailed estimate without any upfront cost. However, many homeowners find these estimates overwhelming, especially when facing other financial pressures.

Texas Disclosure Requirements You Must Follow

Texas law is clear about foundation issues: you must disclose known foundation problems to potential buyers. The Texas Real Property Code requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice, and foundation issues fall squarely under structural problems that must be reported.

Failing to disclose foundation problems can expose you to serious legal liability. Buyers who discover undisclosed foundation issues after closing can sue for damages, rescission of the sale, or both. This applies whether your home is community property with your spouse or solely in your name.

The disclosure requirement applies even if you inherited the property or received it through divorce. If you know about foundation problems, you must disclose them—no exceptions.

What Happens If You Don't Disclose?

Texas courts have consistently ruled in favor of buyers when sellers fail to disclose material defects like foundation problems. You could be liable for repair costs, legal fees, and potentially punitive damages. It's simply not worth the risk.

Your Options for Selling With Foundation Problems

You have three main paths forward when selling a house with foundation problems in Texas:

Option 1: Repair First, Then Sell Traditionally

Some homeowners choose to pay for foundation repairs before listing with a real estate agent. This approach can help you achieve a higher sale price and appeal to traditional buyers who need financing. However, you'll need to front the repair costs (often $10,000-$25,000 or more), wait for the work to be completed, then hope the increased sale price covers your investment plus agent commissions of 5-6%.

Option 2: Sell As-Is to a Traditional Buyer

You can list your home as-is and hope to find a buyer willing to take on the foundation problems. Be prepared for significantly lower offers—buyers typically discount far more than the actual repair cost to account for their hassle and risk. You'll also face challenges with buyer financing, as many lenders won't approve loans on homes with significant foundation issues until repairs are completed.

How Foundation Problems Affect Your Texas Property Value

Foundation issues impact property values differently across DFW depending on your location and market conditions. In high-demand areas like Frisco, Southlake, or Colleyville, buyers may be more willing to negotiate because they want the location. In other areas, foundation problems can make your home nearly impossible to sell through traditional channels.

Traditional buyers with FHA or VA financing often cannot purchase homes with active foundation problems, significantly limiting your buyer pool. Even conventional loan buyers may struggle to secure financing without a structural engineer's report and completed repairs.

Why DFW Foundations Fail: The Clay Soil Factor

The DFW Metroplex sits on some of the most expansive clay soil in the United States. This soil can expand up to 10% when saturated and shrink dramatically during drought. This constant movement—known as differential settlement—causes foundations to shift, crack, and fail over time.

Even properly constructed foundations eventually show stress from our soil conditions. If you're in McKinney, The Colony, Richardson, Grapevine, or any DFW city, your foundation has been battling these conditions since day one.
