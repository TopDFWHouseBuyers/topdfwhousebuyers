The Hidden Costs of Being a Rockwall Landlord

Beyond the obvious expenses like mortgage payments and property taxes, Rockwall rental property owners face numerous costs that reduce their actual return on investment:

Vacancy Periods

Even in a strong rental market like Rockwall, turnover between tenants means lost rental income. The average vacancy period can range from 30 to 90 days, during which you're still responsible for all property expenses, utilities, and ongoing maintenance.

Property Management Fees

If you've hired a property management company to handle your Rockwall rental, you're likely paying 8-12% of monthly rent plus additional fees for tenant placement, maintenance coordination, and lease renewals. These costs add up significantly over time.

Maintenance and Repairs

Rockwall's hot Texas summers are particularly hard on air conditioning systems, and the occasional severe weather can damage roofs, siding, and landscaping. A single major repair like replacing an HVAC system can cost $5,000-$10,000 or more, wiping out months of rental income.

Property Taxes

Rockwall County has seen property tax assessments increase as property values rise. Unlike owner-occupied homes that benefit from the Texas homestead exemption (which caps annual assessed value increases at 10%), rental properties don't receive this protection, meaning your tax bills can jump dramatically from year to year.

Insurance Premiums

Landlord insurance policies cost significantly more than standard homeowner's insurance, and rates have been climbing throughout Texas in recent years.

Texas-Specific Considerations for Rental Property Owners

Selling a rental property in Texas involves some unique considerations that differ from other states:

Community Property Laws

If you're married and acquired the rental property during your marriage, Texas community property laws typically mean both spouses have an ownership interest. Both signatures will generally be required to sell the property, even if only one name is on the title.

Capital Gains Implications

While Texas has no state income tax (a major advantage for rental property owners), you'll still need to consider federal capital gains taxes when selling. The good news is that rental property owners can often deduct depreciation recapture and property improvements. Consulting with a tax professional is always recommended.

Eviction Timelines

If you have problem tenants you need to remove before selling, Texas has relatively landlord-friendly eviction laws compared to some states. However, the process still takes time—typically 3-6 weeks minimum from initial notice to tenant removal, and longer if the tenant contests the eviction.

Selling with Tenants in Place

Texas law requires you to honor existing lease agreements even when selling a property. Many traditional buyers don't want to purchase a property with tenants in place, which can limit your buyer pool and delay your sale.
