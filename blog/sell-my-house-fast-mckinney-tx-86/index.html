Understanding Texas Probate and Inherited Property

Before you can sell an inherited property in McKinney, you'll need to understand how Texas probate laws affect your situation. Texas has specific rules that govern inherited property, and McKinney homeowners must follow these procedures through the Collin County probate court.

The Texas Probate Process

In Texas, most estates must go through probate before property can be transferred or sold. The process typically involves:

  • Filing the will with the Collin County Probate Court
  • Appointing an executor or administrator
  • Inventorying estate assets, including the McKinney property
  • Paying outstanding debts and taxes
  • Distributing assets to heirs

The timeline for Texas probate can range from six months to over a year, depending on the complexity of the estate. However, in some cases, Texas offers simplified probate procedures like muniment of title or small estate affidavits that can speed up the process.

Community Property Considerations

Texas is a community property state, which affects how inherited property is classified. If the deceased owned the McKinney property with a spouse, half may belong to the surviving spouse as community property, while the other half passes according to the will or Texas intestacy laws. Understanding these distinctions is crucial before listing the property.

Step-by-Step Guide to Selling Your Inherited McKinney Property

Step 1: Determine Legal Ownership

Your first step is confirming you have legal authority to sell the property. This means obtaining necessary documents from the Collin County probate court, including letters testamentary or letters of administration. If multiple heirs are involved, you'll need agreement from all parties before proceeding with a sale.

Step 2: Assess the Property Condition

Many inherited properties in McKinney haven't been updated in years or may have been vacant for months. Take time to evaluate:

  • Overall condition of the home
  • Necessary repairs or updates
  • HOA violations or fees (common in McKinney neighborhoods like Stonebridge Ranch, Eldorado, and Craig Ranch)
  • Property tax status and any liens
  • Utility bills and maintenance issues

McKinney's older neighborhoods like Tucker Hill or homes near historic downtown may require significant updates to compete in the traditional market, while newer properties in Craig Ranch or Adriatica Village might be in better condition.

Step 4: Handle Outstanding Obligations

Before closing, you'll need to address:

  • Outstanding mortgage balances

  • HOA fees and violations
  • Utility bills
  • Any liens against the property

Texas law requires these obligations to be satisfied before or at closing. The good news is that Texas has no state income tax, so you won't owe state capital gains taxes on the sale.

Tax Implications of Selling Inherited Property in Texas

One advantage of selling inherited property in Texas is the favorable tax environment. While you won't pay state income tax on the sale, you should understand federal capital gains implications.

Step-Up in Basis

When you inherit property, you typically receive a "step-up in basis," meaning the property's tax basis is adjusted to its fair market value on the date of the owner's death. This often significantly reduces or eliminates capital gains taxes when you sell shortly after inheriting.

For example, if your parent bought the McKinney home for $150,000 years ago, but it was worth $350,000 when they passed away, your basis is $350,000. If you sell for $350,000, you owe no capital gains taxes.

Homestead Exemption Changes

The Texas homestead exemption reduces property tax burden for primary residences. Once the original owner passes away, surviving spouses may continue the exemption, but other heirs typically cannot. This means property taxes may increase while you're deciding whether to sell, adding to holding costs.
