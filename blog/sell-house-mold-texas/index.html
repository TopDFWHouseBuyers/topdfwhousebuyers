Discovering mold in your DFW home can feel overwhelming, especially when you need to sell. The good news is that you have options, even if your property has significant mold issues that would make traditional buyers walk away.

Texas Mold Disclosure Laws You Need to Know

Under Texas Property Code Section 92.052, sellers must disclose known mold issues to potential buyers. This requirement applies to visible mold growth, previous mold problems, and water damage that could lead to mold. Failing to disclose can result in legal liability after the sale. In the DFW area, with our humid summers and occasional flooding, mold is a common concern that cannot be hidden or ignored during a sale.

The Texas Real Estate Commission requires sellers to complete a Seller's Disclosure Notice, which specifically asks about previous flooding, standing water, and water penetration. Any history of moisture problems must be disclosed, as these conditions often lead to mold growth.

The True Cost of Mold Remediation in DFW

Professional mold remediation in the Dallas-Fort Worth area typically costs between $500 for minor issues and $6,000 or more for extensive problems. Large-scale remediation involving structural damage can exceed $30,000. These costs include testing, containment, removal, and prevention measures. Many homeowners in Plano, Frisco, and surrounding cities discover that their insurance does not cover mold damage unless it resulted from a covered peril like a burst pipe.

Beyond remediation, you may face additional expenses for repairs to walls, floors, HVAC systems, and insulation. Traditional buyers often request credits or repairs after inspection, which can delay or derail your sale entirely.

Why Traditional Sales Fall Through

Most conventional buyers in Richardson, McKinney, and Allen rely on financing, and lenders rarely approve mortgages for homes with active mold issues. The appraisal process flags mold as a health and safety concern, requiring remediation before closing. Even FHA and VA loans have strict property condition requirements that mold-affected homes cannot meet.

Real estate agents may also hesitate to list mold-damaged properties, knowing that disclosure requirements and buyer concerns will make showings difficult and offers unlikely.
