Why Traditional Sales Don't Work for Tired Landlords

You might be thinking about listing your Garland rental property with a real estate agent. For landlords dealing with occupied properties or deferred maintenance, this approach creates more problems than it solves.

Challenges of Listing a Rental Property

First, there's the tenant situation. If your property is occupied, coordinating showings becomes a nightmare. Texas law requires you to give tenants reasonable notice before entry, and uncooperative tenants can sabotage sales by keeping the property in poor showing condition or refusing access altogether.

If the property is vacant, you'll need to make it market-ready. Traditional buyers and their lenders expect properties in good condition. That means repairing everything your tenants damaged or neglected—new carpet, fresh paint, landscaping, maybe even major systems like HVAC or plumbing. These repairs can easily cost $15,000-$40,000 or more.

Then comes the waiting game. The average home in Garland takes 30-60 days to sell in normal market conditions, followed by another 30-45 days for the buyer's financing to close. During this time, you're still responsible for all property expenses, and deals fall through regularly when inspections reveal problems or financing doesn't materialize.

Real estate commissions in Texas typically run 6% of the sale price. On a $250,000 property, that's $15,000 gone immediately. Add holding costs, repairs, and closing expenses, and the traditional sale route becomes far less attractive.

Texas-Specific Considerations for Rental Property Sellers

HOA Rules and Rental Restrictions

Many Garland neighborhoods, particularly newer developments near Firewheel Town Center, have homeowners associations with specific rental restrictions. Some HOAs limit the percentage of rental properties allowed, require landlord registration, or impose tenant screening requirements.

If your property is in an HOA with rental caps and restrictions, selling can actually be easier than continuing to navigate these rules. We're familiar with Garland HOAs and can work within their requirements.

Property Tax Implications

Texas property taxes are among the highest in the nation, and Garland properties fall under multiple taxing jurisdictions including Garland ISD, Dallas County, and various municipal utility districts depending on location.

If you've been claiming the homestead exemption on your primary residence, you know this provides significant tax savings—but rental properties don't qualify. Your rental property is taxed at the full rate, which can be substantial. Selling eliminates this ongoing obligation.

Foreclosure Timeline in Texas

If your rental property has become a financial burden and you're behind on mortgage payments, understanding Texas foreclosure timelines is critical. Texas is primarily a non-judicial foreclosure state, meaning lenders can foreclose relatively quickly without court involvement—sometimes in as little as 45-60 days after the first missed payment.

Selling to a cash buyer can help you avoid foreclosure, protect your credit, and potentially walk away with cash instead of facing a foreclosure judgment. Time is essential in these situations, which is why our fast closing timeline can make a critical difference.
